Approach — UX Laws
  • Hick’s LawFewer choices through hierarchy and focus.
  • Fitts’s LawKey actions larger, closer, easier to hit.
  • Proximity LawGroup related information so structure reads.
  • Jakob’s LawFamiliar patterns when they speed users up.
  • Aesthetic–UsabilityVisual polish reads as trust and ease.
  • Miller’s LawChunk and reveal to lower cognitive load.

BMW — The Problem

The starting point: type every search — while driving. BMW’s original in-car search lived on the center-console touchscreen, with an on-screen keyboard.

  • — Drivers search each destination, letter by letter.
  • — Eyes on the screen and a hand off the wheel.
  • — Basic, slow, and distracting in motion.
  • — Far from the premium, driver-friendly BMW standard.

Visual: the original input — typing a destination on the center-console touchscreen.

BMW — My Role & the Voice UI

Role — Visual & motion design for the in-car voice experience

Strategic visual motion and auditory cues kept the driver aware of the search status at all times — eyes on the road, never guessing what the system was doing.

01

Activated & Ready

The system is awake and waiting — a calm, present state that signals it’s ready for a command.

02

Listening

Actively capturing the driver’s voice — motion responds to input so they know they’re being heard.

03

Calculating

Processing and searching — motion communicates progress so the wait never feels uncertain.

Ali SaidBMW · 03 / 04
BMW — Result & Impact
  • — Drivers understood the search status at every moment — visual, auditory, and motion cues working together.
  • — Invisible system states made visible — ready, listening, or working, at a glance.
  • — Less repetition and hesitation, because status was never ambiguous.
  • — A calmer, more trustworthy experience, with minimal distraction.
